Who will pay?
Why would they switch?
Can you deliver it at a sustainable cost?

I built VentureStress AI for Devpost's Build With AI: Basics hackathon, a challenge focused on learning AI best practices by creating a working proof of concept.

For my project, I chose a problem entrepreneurs face early: turning a promising business idea into assumptions they can actually test. The goal was to put those AI practices into a useful product and grow my AI engineering skills along the way.

It gives entrepreneurs a structured starting point for validation:

• Define where the business will serve customers.
• Review and edit 2–3 suggested customer segments, their needs, and who pays.
• Examine assumptions across demand, competition, economics, and operations.
• Identify three priorities and explore practical experiments.
• Research competitor candidates with Tavily and follow the source links.

Each assumption comes with a proposed experiment, a metric, and a decision rule. Competitor results explicitly flag unverified local availability.

My focus with this build: AI that supports a decision and makes its uncertainty visible.

The engineering work included structured-output validation, server-side credentials, recoverable provider errors, and keeping model reasoning distinct from retrieved web evidence.

Built with Next.js, TypeScript, the Google GenAI SDK, Tavily, and Netlify. The current build passes 19 automated tests.

This is a working proof of concept. Customer interviews and real experiments are still needed to test the business hypotheses.
